# Product screen playbook

You are designing a screen inside a paid product, not an admin CRUD. The failure mode this skill exists to prevent: **defaulting to a table of rows with add/edit/delete**, because that is the statistically common shape. Real products are designed around what the user is trying to ACHIEVE and how fast they reach value.

Scope: every screen under `apps/web/src/app/(dashboard)/**` (the authenticated app). Marketing (`(marketing)`) has its own skill. A `PreToolUse` hook loads this automatically on edits here — you never wait to be asked.

**This playbook is project-agnostic.** It holds what any product built on this template needs, including the fixes of defects already met in production, written so a new project does not meet them again. A project writes its own bindings (the product's name, its automation settings, its examples, its copy rules) in `.claude/rules/app-ux.md` and `apps/web/CLAUDE.md`; where a binding is more specific, it is the project's decision.

## Pass 0 — goal, not table

Before any layout, write down: **who** is on this screen, **what job** they came to do, and **what "success" looks like** for them here. Design backward from that. Ask: if this screen were not a table, what would it be? A table is a valid answer only when the job is genuinely "scan/compare/manage many rows" — otherwise it is the lazy default. Prefer: a focused summary + the one primary action; a guided flow; a single object view with its context; a dashboard that answers a question.

## Pass 0b — who does the work: the product or the person

Before designing a screen where a person does a task by hand (writes, fills in, copies, marks, checks something against a rule), ask whether the product can do it from what it already has (a document it holds, an integration, a rule, its AI), and design that instead:

- **The product does the task**, and every automated run is recorded (what ran, on what, with what result). How much it decides alone is the account's setting, per decision: automatic, automatic with a share checked by a person, or a person decides. The project sets the default.
- **The person only answers** what only they know or decide: a question asked in place (a blank only they can fill, a choice left open), a decision, a document to send again after a refusal said with why.
- **The screen shows what the product did**, says beside it, not in a distant help text, that it may be wrong and what to check, and lets the person change it; what it could not do alone waits for a person with the reason.
- **Propose, never ask for a technical fact.** Where the product can find a fact (which system, which code, which category, what a document says), it proposes it and the person approves it or not, the approval recorded as that person's decision. A field asking the person to look the fact up is the defect.

A screen where a person does by hand what the product could do is the exception, and its reason is written beside it.

**Never list the product's work as the person's step.** A checklist, a count, a pending line, an empty state or a message names as the person's only what a person does or decides. What the product does alone is said as the product's: done, being done, or waited for ("Waiting for the first order from your store"), with a manual way, where one exists, offered as the exception and never as the primary action. Failure this prevents: a setup checklist ending with "Create your first record", while the product creates records by itself from an integration, told the user who had just finished the setup that the work was manual.

**A registration starts from documents.** Where a record is already written on a document the person has (an ID, a proof of address, a receipt, a screenshot, a photo of a printed page), the form opens on sending it: the product reads it, fills the fields and marks each as read from the document until somebody writes over it, and the person confirms or corrects; a field no document gives is asked beside them. Typing every field is the contingency ("Fill in by hand"), never the first screen. Failure this prevents: a form of typed fields for a new person whose ID and receipt the product had already read on another screen.

**What the product makes from the person's own material keeps their words.** A template or text the product prepares from a document the person sent keeps that document's wording and layout: only the words that change from one use to the next become placeholders (one per field of a form, never a whole paragraph folded into one), and what the person confirms is theirs (their company, its registration, their signatories) stays as they wrote it, the check for leftovers of the original taking those as theirs. Where what was kept can disagree with what the product does later (a fixed list of signatories against the one it picks), the later step's screen says so. Failure this prevents: a form made from an account's own template came back with the customer's fields folded into one placeholder and the account's own company and signatories replaced by placeholders the product filled with less.

**What the product already knows is compared, never asked.** Before it asks whether something in the person's material is theirs (their company's registration, their staff, their address), the product compares it with the account's own record: what matches is theirs without a question, the check for leftovers takes it as theirs however it is written (dots, case, a country code), and only what the record lacks or contradicts is asked. Failure this prevents: an account sent its own template and was asked whether the company number and the professionals named in it were its own, every one of them already given in its setup.

**An upload says what it found and what it changes.** A record made from an uploaded document opens on the upload; the type, the use and every field the product read are filled and marked as read until somebody writes over them; and where nothing of the account's own covers that use yet, "use this one" comes ticked with one line saying what that changes. Typing it all by hand is the exception a quiet button offers. Failure this prevents: a template the product had already identified and filled waited in a form whose "default" box was unticked, so the account kept writing from the product's generic one.

## The activation principle (from onboarding research)

The best products bring the user to VALUE fast (the "aha moment") — they sell the outcome, not the feature list.

- **First-run is not a blank screen.** A brand-new account must land on a path to value: a welcome + the first meaningful action, or a short setup. Show what the product will do for them.
- **Personalize when it earns its keep**, and then *show what the answer unlocked* — a home already populated with the user's relevant content beats an empty one.
- **Onboarding checklist** (`OnboardingChecklist` + `@flyee/onboarding`): a persistent, dismissible-but-reopenable list of the few steps to first result. Replacing pop-ups/banners with a checklist lifted one-week retention +10% (Mural). It doubles as the honest gamification below.
- **Progressive disclosure** (`SetupWizard`): one decision per screen with a visible progress rail; a long setup that never feels long. Splitting a form across steps can *raise* conversion (House +15%).
- **Never divert attention from a setup**: a setup or onboarding flow holds no exit before it ends, no link to another screen, no "see more", no showcase of features to explore. Everything a step needs happens in the step (a dialog, an inline form, the second factor, an invitation's link copied or sent by e-mail in place), and information for later belongs on the home or its own page. Every link out of a setup is a setup left half done. Failure this prevents: a "See add-ons" line on a setup's first step took the account's owner away before anything was configured.
- **Settle every prerequisite before the step that needs it**: before ordering a flow's steps, list what each step's writes require (a second factor, a role, a connection, data another step writes). Each requirement becomes its own step ahead of the first step that needs it, and the flow does not advance past it until it is met, with the reason on screen. A step that opens disabled, waiting on something an earlier step could have settled, is a dead end. Failure this prevents: a second factor asked inside six setup steps locked the very first one; it became step 1.
- **Split setups by scope.** What every account needs is the core setup; what one module or feature needs is that module's own setup, after the core and before the home, only for what the account has, each in its module's colour, the others shown disabled as what the account could add. Never put a module's parameter in the core, nor ask an account to set up what it does not have.
- **Everybody who joins has a setup of their own.** An invited member is never dropped on a home that says nothing: their setup asks the second factor first, then what their role needs to work (a professional registration, a signature), and the home reminds them of what they set aside.
- **Reassure along the way**: inline validation (a password field that ticks requirements as you type), microcopy that explains the impact of a step, tooltips on dry concepts. None of it is flashy; it makes the flow feel effortless.

## Empty states (non-negotiable)

Every zero-data view uses `EmptyState` — icon + one line of why + the ONE next action (the nudge). A bare "No data" / empty table is a UX failure. The empty state is often a user's FIRST impression of a feature: make it teach and invite. Whether a view is empty is decided by the list it stands for, never by a count of a narrower scope (see "Lists, counts and the latest few"), and a read that failed is an error with a retry, never an empty state.

- **A section with nothing in it is never left as empty space.** It takes a drawing in the product's illustration set (line art in one hue, as the template's empty cards), its title, one line of why and, where there is one, the next action, outlined, since the drawing already draws the eye: the drawing above the words in a card or a column, beside them in a short band across the page (a board with no item, a card of goals with none). A row of empty columns, each saying "nothing here", is one empty state, not many. Pick the drawing by the subject, and keep one per screen region. Failure this prevents: a section beside two cards held one grey line, "nothing here yet", over a blank the height of both cards. In this template: `EmptyState` `illustration`, `layout="row"`, `framed={false}` inside a card.
- **The empty state is where a definition teaches.** What a screen or a section is may be said in full in its empty state (the first visit), and only behind an "i" once there is something in it.

## Engagement patterns — what works vs theater

Distilled from the gamification research — most of it is a WARNING:

- **Use: completion drive.** Progress toward closing an open loop (Gestalt closure — Apple activity rings drove 49.5% behavior change). This is the onboarding checklist + `ActivationProgress` ring. It maps REAL setup/usage progress.
- **Use: competence feedback.** Signals that the user got better at the actual thing (real personal records, meaningful milestones tied to genuine work) — the need most tied to lasting motivation. Domain-specific: design it into the product's own data, not as a bolt-on.
- **Avoid: PBL theater** — points, badges, leaderboards bolted on. The three most-documented failures (LinkedIn, Foursquare, Google News all retired theirs); they drive quantity over the behavior the business needs.
- **Avoid: streak traps.** Streaks shift from motivating to obligational and carry active regulatory risk (EU Digital Fairness Act). Do not ship a streak by default.
- **Avoid the S-curve.** Stacking streaks + points + badges + challenges reverses engagement (Habitica: 100% of studied users had counterproductive effects). If you're stacking mechanics, you're past the peak.
- **Local, winnable competition** beats a global leaderboard when competition fits the product at all (Strava segments).

## Layout & craft (dashboards)

- Arrange by the QUESTION the screen answers, not by table schema. Group related data; decide how much belongs on one screen before it clutters.
- **A definition goes behind an "i", never in a paragraph under a title.** A page, a card or a section opens on its title and what it holds; what it is for sits behind an info button beside the title (a real button, so touch and keyboard reach it), at most about 200 characters, kept by a test that reads every such text where the code writes it. One line under a title is in sight only for state or data that tells the thing apart (how many a list shows, a phone number, the kind of a record) or for what must be read before acting: a consequence, a legal note, that the product may err, personal data, and the instructions a layperson needs on a page of their own. A definition that repeats the title goes away entirely. A sentence that says how to use the screen ("each line opens the list") is replaced by what shows it: a chevron on the row, a hover, a "See in the list" link. A tile is its icon and its name. Failure this prevents: an overview page held a paragraph under its title and under each section, read on every visit, so the screen read as tiring before anything on it was. In this template: `SectionTitle` and `ConsultationStepHeader` take `description` (behind the "i") and `detail` (in sight), and a page's own `h1` puts its definition in an `InfoHint` beside it.
- **A record's screen gets the same pass, card by card.** The rule is not only for overview pages: on the screen of one record made of many cards, each card's sections, empty states and closing notes are read too. A paragraph under a sub-heading inside a card goes behind the "i" beside the sub-heading; a closing note on another way to do the work (a document written outside the product, a paper sent by hand) goes into the card's own "i"; a state line keeps the state and the step the person takes, beside an "i" for the way out of the automation, and drops the sentence that repeats the button beside it; a card that said "none" in a grey line takes a drawing beside its words, and in a narrow column above them. Failure this prevents: once the overview pages were cleaned, the record screen people open most still held about 150 explanatory texts longer than a line, some 3,700 words, because the pass stopped at the pages that had been named. In this template: `SectionTitle` with `variant="subtitle2"` and `as="h3"` inside a card, and an `InfoHint` beside a state line.
- **A summary page lays out by what it holds.** A home or an overview of a few cards whose content varies by the day (a list of what needs the person, a few counts, a line of what the product did) puts the main list in a wide column and the short ones in a side column beside it; a column with nothing to say is not drawn, and the other then takes the whole width. A grid of a fixed number of columns lets its items fit the width (`auto-fit`, or a container query on the card), so one item is never left in half or a quarter of a row; a card that sits now across the page, now in a side column, reads its own width, never the screen's. Failure this prevents: a home whose one-line card, list of one and tile of one each ran across the page, leaving most of it empty.
- **Who owes what, where, and with what.** A card's head says ONE state, in the colour of whose turn it is (the person's, a customer's, the product at work, a failure): never a badge of its own beside a state the screen gives, which put a green check beside "missing" and a customer's clock beside a badge in the account's colour. Everything owed is a line with its owner, its place and its one button beside it: what an outside party owes says who and since when (asked on such a day, opened on another, not sent yet, with the way to where it is sent), with doing it for them as the exception; a fix that lives on another card links there. "Check this" comes with the thing to check in front (the document beside each value read, both values of a mismatch, the source of a choice); a confirm button the person cannot judge in place is a defect. Facts of different kinds go under their names in a grid, never a run joined by a dot. A notice about the environment (a service not switched on here) never reaches a working screen; what the platform still owes is said as such with what it holds. Before asking a person which item a fact is about (which item of a list, which document), let the product decide by a rule it can apply, say it decided and that it may err, and ask only when the rule cannot tell. Failure this prevents: a card whose grey "Missing: marital status, profession" named no one, whose "I checked it against the document" showed no document, and whose head said two different owners.
- **Filters touch what they filter, and a model's chart needs its data.** Cards that filter a list (a funnel, views) sit right above that list: a block of numbers between them pushes the list away from its own controls. A dimension with one or two values does not get a heading over a bar in a card of its own row: dimensions go in tiles of one card (tinted icon column, name, the few largest parts), an even number of them so no row ends with a hole. A count that is a share of a whole may take a ring beside its x/y. When the project's owner sends a template's elements (sparklines, weekly bars, rings, tiles), use each only where the data carries it: counts of now take rings and tiles, never a trend line, which needs a series the payload holds. An empty state that carries the action hides the same button from the head. Failure this prevents: a page whose filters were separated from their list by seven headings of one bar each, most of the card blank.
- **A page that asks someone for several things reads in the order they do them.** One pure, tested rule says what is still owed, in the order of the process, how many each step asks, what is done and whose turn it is; the head's count, the numbered steps and the list of what is done all come from it, so a count never says less than the page asks. Each step is one card with a number, its title, one chip and what it is for behind the "i"; what is done is one line each that opens the section as it was; what the other side asks of an event sits beside that event. Every section shares one frame that is its own card alone or its content inside a step, never three ways of heading a card. Failure this prevents: a page whose "what is still missing" counted four of nine things, whose first instruction pointed at its sixth card, and whose one request sat under seven cards of what was already done.
- **Numbers read from outside are checked by hand before they are drawn as a forecast.** A statistic the product sells as its value (what an outside body decides, what a market pays) is measured against a hand-read sample of its source first, and the screen says its sample and its base; the reading's defects change the very numbers the screen would make attractive. Failure this prevents: a forecast drawn from a reader that took warnings for final outcomes and one kind of amount for another, and searched one of three companies by a name the source never writes.
- **A record inside a card reads in one order.** Head (its name, one state, its edit and its menu), then its facts under their names in a grid ordered so a wide fact closes a row (no hole in the middle), then what is still owed, one line each with its answers beside it, then its foot (a link elsewhere, in the card foot's shape). How the product came to a value (what it read, the rule it applied, that it may err) sits behind an "i" beside that fact's name, never as a paragraph between the facts; a value the product proposes and a person approves is one of the facts, with the approval under it once given, never a box with a second state inside the record. A "Correct" beside the record's own edit is the same action twice. What the product already did is a done line with its way to it ("See in the calendar"), never also offered as a task, and a recommendation is scoped to what it is about ("For this record, the product recommends") so it never reads against the tasks under it; "may err" is said once, behind the "i". Failure this prevents: a record whose facts, four paragraphs of reasons, a nested box with its own chip and three buttons of different weights read as one undifferentiated block.
- **Each kind of action has one place.** On a screen of cards, adding one more to a card's list is a text button with a plus at the card's foot, after its items, on the left; the whole list or its setting elsewhere is a link with a chevron at the foot, on the right; the top of the card keeps only what acts on its record as a whole (editing it). An item's own edit sits at its head beside its state, and what is rare or ends it (remove, revoke) goes in its "⋯" menu, never as buttons strewn at its foot. A form of adding opens from the foot's button, not open at all times; the card's first, main act (sending the first link) may stay in view. One verb for adding across the screen. Put the pattern in a shared card component (props for adding and for the list elsewhere) so a new card cannot place it otherwise. Failure this prevents: "Add items" an outlined button at the top of one card while "+ Add person" sat at the foot of the card above it.
- **Cards side by side line up.** A row of cards whose parts vary (the columns of a board, plans, a card beside another) shares its rows (a CSS subgrid), so every name, number, button and first item sits at one height whatever each card holds, and the cards of a row share their height; an empty one says so in the place of its numbers, keeping its shape. Two cards beside each other take the same kind of head: a section heading over loose cards beside a card with its head inside starts lower and ends elsewhere, and a grid of seven tiles leaves the last alone in its row (lines in a card, in two columns row by row, do not). A card that is itself a grid (a subgrid's rows) gives its one column `minmax(0, 1fr)` and its flex rows `min-w-0`: an `auto` column grows to a text that does not wrap (a long name, `truncate`) and the card clips its numbers and its button. Colour on many columns is a family named over them, never a colour per item when the palette has fewer hues than items. Failure this prevents: a board whose columns started their items at different heights beside a section whose cards broke the line of the card next to it.
- Lead cards/sections with a meaningful icon (show, don't tell — same as marketing). Use the harmonic tones (`components/marketing/tone.ts` hues via tokens) for categorical families; primary for the primary action.
- **Cards lead with their icon and say where they stand.** A card a person works through opens with the icon of what it is on its family's tint, its title, and on the right where it stands (a status chip) and what it keeps one click away (a quick-actions menu); each thing it lists says its state in a state line: done (green, a check), under way (amber, a clock), to look at or act on (the attention accent, an exclamation), failed (red), information (grey), the words saying the state too. In this template: `SectionTitle` (its `chip` and `action`) or, on a consultation card, `ConsultationStepHeader` (its `status` and `trailing`); a quick-actions "⋯" menu and a state line (the state's icon in its colour beside the state in words) are not packaged yet, so build them to this description and register them. A long chip never squeezes the title: when both do not fit, the chip and the menu go to the next line, at the right. Failure this prevents: on a phone, a contact's name became a column of syllables beside a long status chip ("Open until tomorrow, 08:40").
- **A card asks only for what is still owed.** A request already fulfilled (a document that arrived and serves, a link sent, an answer given) is said as done and leaves the card's main area: doing it again goes to the quick-actions menu, one click away; when nothing is left, the card says so first, on a tinted line ("Nothing else to send"). What is still owed, or arrived and does not serve, is a line in the attention colour with its ONE button beside it. Check every card for a button that asks for what already happened. Never hide in the menu the only way to do the card's pending job, nor a right the person must find in plain sight (withdrawing a consent, deleting their data). Failure this prevents: a link page asked a person to send an ID a partner had already sent for them.
- **One line per thing, its state on the right.** A card's list is one row per thing: what it is and whose on the left; its state in words on the right, aligned down the card (a state line); a button only on a row that still owes something; a detail under the name (a refusal's reason, a file's name) only where it explains the state or tells two rows apart. A box per item with a chip, a second chip and a button each doubles the card's height for nothing.
- **Confirm a binding act in front of the person.** An act whose consequences the person must understand (a consent given or withdrawn, a decision others will act on) ends in a dialog that states the outcome (the state in a large icon, the title in their words, one consequence a line, one button), never in an alert or a toast at the top of the page, which is read past. Such an outcome dialog is not packaged here (`ConfirmActionDialog` is the confirmation before an operator's act, not this): build it to this description.
- **A tag beside a name in a narrow column takes the name's room.** The state of an item of a menu or a narrow list (coming soon, locked, new) floats over the item's top edge at its far end, as the count on a notification bell floats off its corner, never crowding the icon, and is said in the item's own accessible name; the name keeps one line, the column wide enough for the names it holds today, an ellipsis only past it. Failure this prevents: an inline chip beside each module name in a 240px submenu broke every name into two lines.
- **Every registration opens in a panel at the side of the page.** A new record and its edit open in one side panel: the page behind under a veil, the head with the icon of what is made and one line of what happens to it, the form scrolling, the main button and "Cancel" kept at the foot. It opens from the card the record belongs to and from an "Add" at the top, which lists by group only what this person may add and, opened away from the record's home, asks first for what the record is of (a customer, then which of their records). Never a centered dialog for a registration, nor a page of its own. A shared side panel is not packaged here: build it to this description and register it.
- **A panel opens with the focus on its first field.** A drawer or a dialog takes the focus to its frame as it opens: once it is in place, put the person on its first field, and give the focus back to what opened it when it closes; a menu item that opens a panel opens it after the menu finished closing, or the menu hands the focus back to its button over the panel. A drawer created already open runs no entrance transition (it animates only when `open` changes after it mounted), so a focus placed "when the entrance ends" never comes: place it once the panel is on the page too, skipping a file chooser kept out of sight and a field that is only read. A choice among options starts on the option chosen. A field inside such a panel never takes `autoFocus` of its own: the drawer moves the focus to its frame as it opens, the field blurs, and a form that validates on blur says "required" before the person typed anything. Failure this prevents: the "Add" panel opened with the focus on its frame and what the person typed went nowhere; every panel opened from a card or a row menu kept the focus on its frame, which only a panel opened by toggling `open` escaped; and an invitation opened saying "enter the e-mail".
- **A submenu never stays open over the page.** It opens on its group's icon and closes on the same icon, on a click away and on following any of its links, also one that stays on the path (a `#` link), which a reset on a new path never sees. Opened again, it marks the page the person is on. Closed, its links leave the tab order too (`inert`), since a panel of no width still holds them. Failure this prevents: a submenu open by default on wide screens covered the left of every page of its group, while the page reserved room only for groups written in the static menu.
- **A group's own page is a link beside its submenu's title, never an item of it.** When a menu group with children also has a page of its own (the overview of all of them), the submenu's title carries a subtle link to it ("All", or the page's own word, as "Overview"), and on a phone's collapsible list the same link sits on the group's row, beside the toggle, never inside it. Failure this prevents: an "All modules" item at the head of the submenu took a line and read as one more module.
- **Every list has the same bar.** Above any list: the ready views with how many each holds, grouped in one menu; the list's search; "Filter" with the list's own filters, which narrow every count and view the same way; "Sort"; "Export" only where the list really exports (a button that exports nothing is a fake surface); what the list keeps out of sight (opening a record by hand, an import) at the right; the filters in force as chips under it. Whoever learns one list knows them all. A shared list bar is not packaged here: build it to this description (where the list is the `DataTable`, its `header`, `filters`, quick filter, `actions` and `enableExport` carry the same parts).
- **Every registration is managed the same way.** The list has the bar and, at its right, "New": one button for one kind of record, one button with a menu for several, the one that starts from a document first; a list of what the product makes by itself has no "New" (a manual one is the exception, in the bar's "More actions"). Creating and editing happen in the side panel, the document first where there is one, and a row opens its record by its name, with the rest of what it allows one click away in a quick-actions menu. A record another record uses is archived with its reason and can come back (listed in a view of its own, with the way back), never deleted under it; what nothing uses is deleted only after a confirmation that says what it does and what stays. Every change is audited; a registration that moves money or binds the account asks the second factor. Import and export by spreadsheet where the person brings or takes many records at once. A long text a person reads and writes (a template) takes the panel's wide form, never a centered dialog.
- **A number is compared with the same span.** A card's big number says what it was before only against the same span: the same days of the month before (the 1st to the 7th against the 1st to the 7th), the end of the same day; never a few days of this month against a whole month, which reads as a fall. The move is green only when it is the one the card hopes for; any other move is grey, never red (red is risk and failure). Each card opens the list it counts, and its number is that list's count. A comparison the data cannot give yet is left out, never invented. In this template: the catalog's `template` "Tile horizontal com variação" (a counter with a previous period), with the comparison in a pure, tested function.
- **A badge is no tap target.** The rule that grows controls to 44px on a phone must skip a state drawn as a label (a badge rendered with a button's styles, marked as presentation): otherwise every badge of a list grows into a tall box on a phone.
- MUI v9 first, theme tokens only (never hardcode colors), Formik+Yup for forms with inline errors near the field, next-intl for every string.
- **One action at a time on a record, said where the person acted.** On a screen of many cards that each write to the same record, a click that writes locks every other control that writes until the screen shows what came of it: the button turns and says what it does ("Creating the records"), the other controls of every card wait disabled and say why when pointed at, and what only reads stays free (opening and closing a card, opening a file, copying a link). The lock lifts only once the record read again is on screen, so a second click never acts on what is about to change, and the focus goes back to the button that had it. What came of it is said in the card where the person acted, with its time, for a few seconds; another card it changed is ringed for a moment and one "See" away in a toast; a failure stays in the card with what to do, and a toast says it only when that card is out of sight. The record's top says the action under way with a thin bar, dims what may change and changes at the same moment as the cards, never after them, marking for a few seconds what changed ("New"); a change made outside (the product's own work, another person) is read again saying "Updating" with nothing locked, and a write that comes by itself never disables the field the person is typing in. How: one state of the screen's actions handed to every card (a context), a router refresh inside a transition whose end lifts the lock, each card's body inside a fieldset disabled while an action runs (a control that only reads drawn as no form control, so it stays free), and a small hook that compares a signature of each line and card with the previous reading to mark what changed. Failure this prevents: on a record of seventeen cards, a click in one left every other card's buttons live, nothing said the action ran nor that it ended, and the record's top kept the old state for seconds after the cards had changed.
- **A list that piles up inside a card is one line per thing, what asks first.** Where a card of a record lists things that accumulate over its life (what an outside party published, documents, messages sent, papers to sign) and only some of them ask the person for something, each thing is one line: its icon, what it is and when, and on the right where it stands in its state's colour and words. What asks for the person comes first, in a section of its own with its count on the card's head, each line saying what is missing and holding its one button (an acknowledgement given in place; what needs reading opens the line); what the product is doing is a line that waits, with no button; what is settled goes below, the newest first, a few in sight and the rest one click away. A line opens in place on everything the thing holds, one at a time, kept mounted once opened so nothing typed there is lost; arriving at the card from a "resolve" link opens the first line that asks. Where each thing stands is a pure rule with a test, never decided inside the markup. A list worked in volume on a page of its own may keep its open cards. How: one shared component for the lines (sections, a state on the right, one action or an opener, a body that opens in place), fed by a pure function per kind of thing that says its place, its state and its action. Failure this prevents: every notice of a record arrived open, one under the other with its full text and its buttons, and the professional had to read all of them to find the one that asked for their acknowledgement.
- **A field in a row of buttons drops the theme's margin for helper text.** A theme that spaces form controls for their helper text puts a field placed in a toolbar a few pixels above the buttons beside it; take the margin off there, and say a search's hint or error on a line under the bar. Failure this prevents: a list bar's search sat 8px above its buttons on every list.
- **A state chip names what decides the next step.** A record that is in one state but held by another thing (ready, but blocked) shows the hold in its chip and is never counted under the first state, so the card that counts "ready" and the chip agree. Failure this prevents: an order something kept from shipping said "Ready to ship" beside the words "blocked: see the record".

## Lists, counts and the latest few

- **A configuration says what is in force for each use, before its list.** Where the product picks one of several records for each use (the template it writes each kind of document from, the paper it sends), the screen opens on one tile per use with the record in force, whether it is the account's own or the product's default, and the way to send the account's own; a use the product splits further (by the kind of record, by the kind of document within it) says its split on the tile. What waits for a person (a question, a result to check, a refusal, a gap) comes first, one line and one button each; the full list after. Failure this prevents: a library listed every template with its chips while nobody could tell which one the product wrote each document from, nor where to replace it.
- **A screen says each thing once.** Two sections fed by the same counts split them, never repeat them: the list of what is late or blocks the work names its items one by one, and the tiles beside it keep only the rest (what falls today, what is good to look at). An empty state ("nothing waits") never sits beside a list that holds items, nor beside the error that says a read failed; and a reminder waits while a checklist on the same screen already asks the same thing. Failure this prevents: a home that listed approvals, incoming notices and overdue deadlines in its "act now" list and again as tiles under "what waits for you", and said "nothing waits" under a list of blocked records.
- **A count counts what its list shows.** A tile, badge or heading count beside a list has the list's scope and filters. A count that leaves something out on purpose (only what still needs action) says so in its label, and never decides whether the list shows. Failure this prevents: a page decided "nothing here yet" from a count that left out items already handed on, and hid the account's only item, which the list itself still held.
- **A list lives on one page; the summaries open it.** Where several pages need the same records (an overview for managers, a home, a module's or a team's area), one page holds the list with its views, search and export, and the others show their summary (numbers, a board, the latest few), each number, column or step opening that list with the same cut (the scope, a filter the summary applied), the cut shown there as a removable chip, so the list has the size the number said. Failure this prevents: an overview page and a module's area each embedding the full list of the work page, its views and export repeated three times, and nobody sure which page to work from.
- **One concept, one rule, on every screen.** When two screens say the same thing of a record (its step, who has it, whether it is late), they read the same rule from the same place; a second rule computing the same word drifts, and the count of one screen opens a list of another size. Failure this prevents: the home counted a step by the record's stage while the board and the record's own top said the step by what waited on it, so the home's 4 opened a list of 10.
- **Each setting has one home, by whose it is.** Settings group by who they belong to: the person's own (true in any organization they join: their profile, security, professional credentials), the whole organization's (changed by who decides for it: its data, team, the rules its records follow, what the product decides alone, what its customers read, its connections, its plan), and each add-on module's (what only that module's records read). Each lives on exactly one page; a work screen or a setup that needs it says how it stands and links to that home, or renders the home's own component in place, never a second control with rules of its own. The rules the organization's records follow sit apart from what the product decides by itself, and a long list of decisions is grouped by the stage of the work each acts in. Failure this prevents: a person's credentials inside the team page, the organization's weekly report on a personal page, one page name used for two pages, two switches for one automated triage, the organization's deadline in a personal panel, and a module's values in the core's settings.
- **Parts add up to their whole, and a name means one thing.** A breakdown (bars by step, counts by owner) lists every bucket that holds items, including a rare one at the end of the way, so its parts sum to the total beside it; and two views of one menu never share a name. When a new concept takes a word ("with the customer" as who has the record), the older view that used it for something else (a stage) is renamed. Failure this prevents: bars by step left out the records paid and not archived yet, and a menu offered "With the customer" twice, a stage and an owner, with different counts.
- **A number that is not counted yet is not a zero.** Where the product cannot count something yet, the screen says so once and draws no bar or number at zero, which reads as nothing done.
- **A saved record is shown where it landed.** When what was just saved belongs in another view of the list (a period still to come, under "current"), the list goes to that view; the panel closing over a list that did not change reads as nothing saved.
- **An empty choice is an option like the others.** A select whose empty value means "all" shows it as chosen, with its label above ("All kinds"), never only the label inside the field, which reads as nothing chosen.
- **An end state belongs to the thing that reached it, never to its owner.** When one part of a record finishes its course (one order of a customer shipped, one request handed on to another party), its state, its chip and its withdrawn actions are that part's; the owner record and its other parts stay open for what comes next (new parts, updates, the consent the next part needs), and every screen that names the owner's state (a list, a home, the outsider's own page) reads it from what is ahead of them, the finished part said beside it. Failure this prevents: a customer whose first order was handed on to a partner had their whole record locked, so their next order could not be agreed to, its documents could not be sent and it was never followed; and their own page would have told them their data had gone elsewhere right after they agreed to the next one.
- **A home shows the latest few, never the list.** The latest 3 to 5 items, one compact line each: what it is with one word under it, on the right its step in words with its progress in small, the whole line a link to the item; then "See all" to the full list. A full list or a table on a home is the defect. Such a list of the latest few is not packaged here: build it to this description and register it.
- **A list a person works through stays still.** While they act on its rows (triage, approve, decide), each row keeps its place whatever the server reads behind it, and they ask for the next ones. A refresh that reorders rows under the pointer makes the person act on the wrong item.
- **A search for a person travels in the request, never in the address.** A name, an ID number or a record number typed in a search goes in the request body: an address lands in history, logs and referrers.
- **A list that loads only its latest rows counts and filters over all of them.** When a list reads its N most recent rows, its views, counts and search are computed where every row is (the database), never over the N shown, and the list says when it shows fewer than its view holds ("The 60 most recent of 132"). The view and the order go in the address, so a home's tile opens the view it counts and an item opened from a view keeps it; the search stays in the request body. Failure this prevents: an inbox read only its 60 latest conversations, so a person who wrote a month ago and was never answered was in no view at all, and a search for a customer looked only among the 60.
- **A name in a menu is read whole.** A menu of views or options wraps a long name, never cuts it: what tells two apart may be its last word. Failure this prevents: three views read "Not moved for more than 3...", "4..." and "9...", the days that made them different cut off.
- **A person's way out is in plain sight, and says what it carries.** Taking everything out of the product is a right, so it is a visible button for who decides for the account, never an item in a menu. A file with everybody's personal data asks the second factor in place, says so before it starts, tells the account's other managers once it is taken, and says on its own first page what it is, when it was made and that it holds personal data, so a copy passed on still says so. A retry button shows only where trying again can help: a daily limit or a file past its size answers the same next time, and a button that can only fail again is the defect.
- **An export is begun on the record, and is whole or nothing.** Who may take data out is the database's to say, with a daily limit; the record keeps what was taken and whether a search narrowed it, never the words searched; the file is written where the person is, from the very list they are looking at (its view, search, filters and order), and nothing is saved of a list that could not be read whole, since half a list read as the whole of it is worse than none. A refusal says why and what to do. A file made to be printed says on its first page what list it is (whose, which view, how many, when, what narrowed it), repeats its column titles and numbers its pages, since a printed sheet travels without the screen; and a format that holds less (a PDF against a spreadsheet) has its own limit as a setting, its refusal naming the format that holds more.
- **A state a panel counts is the record's own rule, proven equal.** When a list or a panel says something a record's screen also says (who has it, its step, whether it is late), the database computes it for every record by the very rule of the record's screen, and a test runs the record screen's own read on fixtures and compares the two record by record. A second rule written for the panel drifts, and the panel and the record then name different people. Failure this prevents: a stock panel would have counted a record as "with the customer" while the record itself said "with the team", the link to the customer being owed.
- **A name in a sentence is not a label.** A type's label reads as a title ("Identity document"); inside a sentence it needs words of its own ("checking the identity document of ..."). Failure this prevents: "The product is checking Identity document of Mariana".
- **A page past the end of its list shows its last page**, never an empty page that reads as "nothing here".
- **A work list reads in the order of the work.** The state of the source the list comes from is one compact card first (its state on its head, closed; open by itself only when the team has something to put right there), then the filters, then the list's own head (which list, one line of what it holds, its search beside it), a legend one click away, and the items. Each item reads top to bottom as it is worked: what it is and where it stands (the icon of its kind, its state in a chip), its facts in one line, the content itself set apart on a tint, the one question the person answers, and at its foot the context (which record, to whom) with what it seldom needs in a menu. Failure this prevents: a list screen had an alert, three tiles, a search card, a status card and a legend before its first item, and each item had its actions in three rows around its text.
- **The few latest things the product did are compact cards, not a list of checks.** One outlined card each, a few across, the whole card the way to see it (a chevron, no button): the icon of what it is about on its family's tint, what was done, and under it one quiet line with when and what else the record says (how many times, a detail, the AI only when it was the AI); the same kind of thing is one card with its count. Failure this prevents: three grey lines of green ticks with "See" drifting at the right edge, two of them saying the same, and the one with no place to go breaking the column; and then three tall cards, each with a time pill, a count pill, an author footer and a "See" button, that made the top of a record read as noise.

## Async work, said while the person is still there

- **Say the outcome where the person acted, while they still have the thing in hand.** What can be checked at once is checked at once (a photo too blurry to read is said under the line it was sent from, with "take another" and "send it as it is": a rule may be wrong, and the person decides). What the server checks later is read again for a bounded while (every ~10 s for a few minutes after the upload), so the answer, a refusal with its reason included, shows before they leave.
- **Status that moves on its own is read again while it moves**: on a visible tab only, at a pace that matches how fast it changes, slower as it waits, stopping when nothing moves or after a bound. The newest answer wins, and a line never says "working" after the work finished.
- **Focus follows the work; the page moves only to show progress the person caused.** When an async action ends, put the person back on the line they acted on (its first button that can be pressed, or the line itself), unless they moved on to type elsewhere. The page never scrolls under them, with one exception: when what they did moved the record along (its steps, who has it, what it waits for), the screen goes to the record's progress panel and marks it for a moment, the focus going back to the button without scrolling and what came of it kept one "See" away in a toast; never from under a field being typed in, and not when the panel's head is already in the upper half of the screen. Failure this prevents: a person acted in a card far below a record's progress and never saw that what they did had moved the record on.
- **A screen where the product works in the background follows it by itself.** One watcher per screen, never one interval per card: it reads a light mark of what the screen shows (a hash the database computes over the rows the screen reads, plus whether the product is at work there now) every few seconds while work is under way or right after the person acted, less often otherwise, never on a hidden tab and at once when it comes back; the screen is read whole only when the mark changed. The screen says when it last knew it was current ("Updated at 14:32, this screen updates by itself"), and that it could not check when a read fails. A card-level interval that only starts when the last read already showed work misses everything the product starts by itself after the person's action. Failure this prevents: a user watched a record while the product created its parts, wrote the document and built the file in seconds, and the open screen showed none of it until a reload; two cards polled on their own and stopped after a few minutes while the work went on.
- **While the product works on a record, the record's screen waits, and is never trapped.** When the product changes a record by itself (writes its text, checks its files, sends it somewhere), a person acting on the same screen at that moment acts on what is about to change: a notice over the page holds its actions, says what the product is doing with its steps (done, under way with how long, what always follows) and when it started, and closes when the work ends, the screen read again behind it. Two safeguards: it holds only for the product's own work, which takes seconds or minutes, never for a wait on a person or an outside party; and from about two minutes on it says the work takes longer than usual and offers "Continue without waiting" (focused) and "Wait more", counting from the work's own start so a stalled work never holds a later visit for two more minutes. Remember per tab what the person went on without, and never open the notice over a field being typed in or another open window. Such a notice is not packaged here: build it to this description and register it.
- **Work that stopped is tried again before a person is called.** A background reading or sync that gives up is retried by the product on a schedule, a few times, and only the last failure becomes a person's alarm; meanwhile the record's card says it stopped, when the next try is and that the person will be told if it does not work, and keeps the person's own way to try now. Failure this prevents: every reading of an outside source that failed once was an alarm on the home, for a fault the source fixed by itself days later.
- **A number moves in its own unit.** The move under a number ("+9%", "+2 h") is said in the unit the number is shown in, or as a share; never in the unit it is counted in underneath. Failure this prevents: a card of hours showed "+3.347" under "56 h", the difference in minutes.
- **A spinner means work under way, nothing else.** A turning mark (on a step, a banner, a chip) shows only while the product is doing something there now; a step that waits for a person keeps its own icon in the colour of "under way". Failure this prevents: two spinners turned on every open record, so the user could never tell when the product was actually working.
- **The top of a record says where it stands, whose turn it is, and what is happening now, once.** Three short parts under its steps: what the product is doing at this moment (each with since when), what holds the record and whose turn each thing is (the team, with which role acts and the way to it for who may; the outsider; the platform's operator when only it can act, said as "nothing for you to do"), and what the product did a moment ago, each line one click from the card where it is done. Who holds the record is computed from those lines (the team before the product's own work, then the outsider, then the platform), from one table of owner per wait shared by every screen that shows waits. Each part says each thing once, with one signal: a line is its state's icon, its sentence, whose turn it is in plain words under it and its one button; no count chips over a list that already names each line, no frame around a list inside the card, no badge that repeats the words beside it. Failure this prevents: a record's top with an owner chip, a count chip, a framed box and a button for a single blocking line read as clutter; and a record said "with the product" while a file waited for a professional to send it, "with the customer" while a signed paper waited for the team to confirm it, and "with the team" while only the platform could act, so the professional hunted through seventeen cards for what was blocking.
- **A decision is taken where its context is, and every notice of it leads there.** What the product proposes about a record (a document, a text, a reply in a conversation) is decided on that record, beside what it is about; a cross-record list of decisions is a convenience for whoever reviews many in a row, reached from the pending center, never the only door and never a main-menu destination, and it never holds a decision that cannot be taken in it. A line of the home or of the pending center names one decision and opens the exact card or conversation, never the list. Failure this prevents: a notification of the home opened a list whose card for a document's text could not approve it, and sent the person on to the record.
- **A card says on its head where it stands, always: done, being worked on now, waiting for the outsider, waiting for the team, failed, or nothing yet**: a short badge in the state's words and tone, and the card's icon in the state's colour with the state's own mark in its corner (a check, a turning mark, a clock, an exclamation, a cross), so a screen of closed cards reads as a list of where things stand; the card's own chip, when it names what asks for somebody more precisely, stands in place of the badge. The card opens by itself when something comes to wait for the team there, and closes once it is done, unless the person opened it or is typing in it; a card that opens once on arrival and never again hides what came to wait while the screen was open. Failure this prevents: closed cards said only "6 documents · 6 accepted" or "Authorization: signed" in grey, and the user could not tell at a glance which were done and which waited for whom.

## Waits that end, and checks that can be given

- **A request to check has a way to say it was checked.** Whatever the screen marks for a person to look at (data the product read, a suggestion, a figure to confirm) has its one action beside the mark ("Checked against the document"), and saving the record as it is counts as that look; the mark then goes and the history says who checked. A mark only an edit clears stays forever on data that was right. Failure this prevents: a person saved a record twice, unchanged, to "validate" what the product had read, and the card kept asking to check it.
- **A wait on someone outside has a way forward for the team.** When the product waits on a third party (a customer's answer, a signature, a document) before its next step, the screen says what it waits for and who answers, and the person who may decide gets "go on without it" beside it, with what that costs said before they confirm (what is left for a person to complete later). A wait only the outsider can end is a dead end the moment they stop answering. Failure this prevents: a record waited for a customer's answers with nothing the team could do but wait, and the user read it as the product failing to write the document.
- **A wait holds only while it holds something.** A line that says the product waits for an answer before its next step stops saying so once that step happened another way (the team went on, the product moved on without it): it then says where the answer goes when it comes, and the way to "go on without it" leaves with the wait. And what the product made before the answer came is made again by the product when the answer arrives, while nobody of the team touched it, so the answer reaches the output instead of a blank for a person. Failure this prevents: a card told the team the product would create the record's parts only after the customer's answers when they already existed, and the document already written never read the account of events that came the next day.
- **"Done" never claims the next step started while it still waits on the person.** A confirmation screen ("You told us you finished") says what is still owed by them, and only then what happens next. Failure this prevents: a customer was told "the team is preparing your request" right after finishing, while it waited for answers only they could give.
- **What this environment cannot do is never shown as pending.** A state that waits on an integration not connected here ("not checked" when no lookup exists) is left out of the line, with one sentence elsewhere saying the integration is off; a grey "not checked" badge reads as a validation the person owes.
- **An alarm is only what a person can put right.** A count on a home, a pending line or a red "late" names a thing only when somebody on the team has an action for it, and its line leads to that action; what the product retries by itself (a sync that is late, an outside source out of reach) is said where it is read, as information ("the product tries again by itself; until then the list may be incomplete"), and watched by whoever runs the platform; and where this environment never does the work (a source switched off here), the screen says only that, with no status that reads as late. Failure this prevents: a home flagged in red "readings to check" for every account of an environment that never reads the outside source, and the page it led to had nothing anybody could do.
- **Many facts from a person outside: one question at a time, where they already are.** When a document or an output needs several facts only a person outside the team knows (a customer's account of what happened), ask in the channel they are in, one question per message, the next only after the one before; leave out what the product already knows (a record, a document read, an earlier answer) and say why on the team's screen; take "I don't remember" as an answer; ask a reply that does not serve once more with an example, then move on; remind once; and offer the same questions one per screen on their page. Each answer is kept as a fact the next step reads (a yes or a no, a moment, their words). Failure this prevents: five questions sent in one message went unanswered, and the document came back with ten blanks for the team to fill.
- **An output the product made keeps a blank for a person only where it cannot stand without the fact.** The product says which facts are essential (data, by kind of record), writes any other missing fact at the detail it was given or leaves it out, and lists what it left out for the reviewer; what an essential blank needs is asked of the person outside, one question at a time, and the output is made again with the answers, once they all came and only while nobody of the team edited it. Ask only of the latest output: a version older than the person's latest answers is remade first, so nothing they already answered (or answered as "I don't know") is asked again, and a follow-up never falls back to the whole questionnaire. While the product carries the output, its card says so ("the product asks the customer", "the product writes it again") and offers no review or approval of the text it is about to replace; every earlier answer stays in sight beside the questions of the round asked now, and the round's count counts only its own questions. Failure this prevents: a document came back with six blanks, each a fact only the customer knew, and a professional had to fill every one by hand; then a second round of seven questions, read from the stale version, asked the customer again what they had just said, while the card asked a professional to review the text being replaced.
- **When the product takes over a step a person did by hand, their steps step back.** While the product does it (or is about to), the card says so and where it stands, and the manual steps and their form leave the card; they come back as the contingency only where the product cannot do it here (no channel, an outside system out of reach for good) or what it did came back to a person. An act whose outcome may have reached an outside system and could not be read (a submission, a payment, a message) is never repeated by itself: the person checks the outside system first, and the screen asks them to say so before it goes again. Failure this prevents: a professional kept following seven manual steps on a card while the product was about to submit the same request, and a lost answer would have submitted it twice.
- **What an automation needs of a person is asked when the work starts, not when the automation reaches it.** A permission, an authorization or a credential a later automated step depends on, above all one that expires (a signing session of a few days, a token, a certificate), is asked from the moment the work it will serve exists: in the center of what waits, on the person's own card (where renewing before it ends is the card's main action, with their last choice kept) and on the record it will serve; its durable part (an identifier, a registration) is asked once in the setup, optional where the automation may never be needed. An outcome of the automation that came back to a person (refused, failed, sent with no answer) is a line of the center of what waits, never only a line of its record. Failure this prevents: an automated submission stopped at its last step for an authorization that lasts a week and was asked only once the file was ready, and a submission the outside system refused was said only on its record.
- **A state that may already have changed is never said as unchanged.** While what may say it changed waits for a person (a message nobody triaged, a document nobody read), the screen says the change may be in it and what follows once it is read, never that it did not happen. Failure this prevents: a record said its appointment had not been scheduled while the outside notice that scheduled it waited, unread, in the inbox of notices.
- **An automation that acts on its own classification asks a person's awareness after it, not their word before it.** Where the product classifies what arrives and acts on it (an incoming notice triaged, a document accepted), and a person's gate would hold the action until somebody looks, apply what the model is sure of at once, leave to a person what it is not, and ask the person responsible to confirm they are aware: on the record's own screen, in the center of what waits until it is given (late after a set time), and again by the bell; the confirmation and the correction are one click each, and a correction re-runs what followed. And everything of a record is worked on the record's own screen: an account-wide list keeps only what has no record, the health of the integration that feeds it, and the work of going through volume. Failure this prevents: an appointment an outside party set waited for a person to say it was an appointment before the product scheduled it and told the customer, while the notice already said so in plain words.

## Never lose what the person typed

- An editor or a dialog with unsaved input asks before closing; a background refresh never closes or resets it; what was typed is kept in the tab and offered back when it is opened again.
- A side trip to settle a prerequisite returns to the form with what was typed (`product-verify` walks it).

## Words, colour and time

- **Hues go on icons, bars and fills; words keep the text colours**: a palette hue rarely reaches 4.5:1 as small text on a light ground.
- **Red is for failure and risk only.** What the person should look at or do takes the attention accent; under way, amber; done, green; information, grey. Every state is said in words too, never by colour alone.
- **A missing value hides its line**: never print "—", "null" or an empty label in its place.
- **One name for the one doing the work.** The product's automated work is said under one name everywhere on the team's screens ("the product checks", "the product writes"), never the product in one card and "the AI" in the next; a decision the product took alone is marked as its own, with "it may err" beside it.
- **A manual change of a record's stage is the exception, and says what it switches off.** Where the product moves the stage by itself, changing it by hand lives in a quick-actions menu, the dialog says per destination what changes (the outsider's link stops taking files, the product stops carrying the record, readings and notices stop), and the outcome is confirmed in front of the person.
- **A time carries its zone**: said in the zone of the place it belongs to (an office, a store, a venue), the zone named when it may not be the reader's.
- **"Corrected" means the words changed.** Where the product read a value and the system stores it normalized (a name read in capitals written as a name, extra spaces collapsed), the screen compares the two the way the system normalizes them (case, spacing, accents) before saying anybody corrected what was read; a plain string comparison tells every record it was corrected. Failure this prevents: every bill whose holder the product read in capitals said under it that the name "was corrected", when nobody had touched it.
- **Interpolated names read as a sentence**: no category word before a name that may already carry it ("the store Store Sol"), no label's capital kept inside a sentence, no two cards of one screen with the same title.
- **An error says its real reason.** A refusal from a provider is read by its code and said as what happened (a sending limit reached, an e-mail that could not leave, an address that already has an account), with what the person can do; "check your connection" only for a request that never reached the server. Failure this prevents: a sign-up refused because the provider's e-mail sender hit its hourly limit told the person to check their connection, and they kept retrying.
- **A one-time code verifies on its last digit**: typed or pasted, the code submits itself once complete, and a wrong one clears for a new try.

## Next.js App Router pitfalls (each one broke a screen in production)

- **Show a required step in place, never by `redirect()` from a page under the shell's Suspense boundary**: in a production build that redirect goes through the client router and the screen breaks (React error #310). Render the step where the page would be.
- **After a sign-in, a sign-out or a change of account, load the next page anew** (a full navigation), never a client-router push: the router keeps the layout's redirect of the old session and replays it on every page under it, leaving a blank screen.
- **A server file imports from a `"use client"` file only its components and its types.** Any other value exported there (a guard like `isStep()`, a list of steps, a constant) reaches the server as a client reference: it typechecks and builds, and throws when the page is requested ("Attempted to call ... from the server but ... is on the client"), so every visit lands on the error boundary. Put what the page and its view both read in a plain module beside them, imported by both.
- **A component that reads its own data takes an optional first read as a prop** (`initial`): a server page can hand it one, and a preview or a test draws it from fixtures without a session.
- **A server action is a public endpoint**: validate every argument as if a stranger sent it, and check the caller's right to act again inside it.

## Forms & semantic fields

- **Semantic data never goes in a raw `TextField`.** Phone, CPF/CNPJ, CEP/postal code and money use the field catalog in `components/product/fields/` (`PhoneField`, `CpfField`/`CnpjField`/`CpfCnpjField`, `CepField`, `CurrencyField`) — mask, inline check-digit validation and locale-aware formatting come built in (`product-lint` advises when it spots a bypass).
- **CEP auto-fill pattern**: `CepField`'s `onAddressFound` pre-fills street/district/city/state — those fields MUST remain editable (a head start, not a lock), a failed lookup silently degrades to manual entry (never block the form on a third-party), and a form that opens with a postal code already filled does not look it up again over the address on record.
- **Persist digits, not masks**: run `onlyDigits()` (`@flyee/fields`) before writing to the DB; validate in Yup with the same package's validators (`.test("cpf", t(...), isValidCpf)`) so UI and schema agree.
- **Inline, translated feedback**: each field takes `invalidMessage` (product namespace) and flags complete-but-invalid values as the user types — never only on submit.
- Respect the loading and error states — skeletons that reserve space (no layout shift), errors that say what to do next.
- **A page that is loading is drawn in its shape, in its own place.** Every route of the signed-in area has a loading state in the form of the page it leads to (a list: its number cards, its bar and its rows; a record: its top and its cards in their columns; a home; a list beside what is open of it; a setup: the rail beside the step), with the same cards and spacing as the real page, said once to assistive technology and still for whoever asks for less motion. Never a spinner in the middle of the screen, nor one over the header and menu: it says nothing of what is coming, and the frame of the app is already there. A page skeleton is not packaged here: build one to this description, used in each route's `loading.tsx`, the group's own `loading.tsx` catching the rest. Failure this prevents: every page of the signed-in area opened on one spinner fixed over the whole screen, menu included.

## Component conformance (check BEFORE writing markup)

`apps/web/src/components/product/catalog.json` is the machine-readable catalog of this layer, and the `product-screen-guard` hook injects a digest of it into every write/edit here — so the full range is always in context. Two kinds of entry: `components` already exist (import them) and `template` are flyee patterns not packaged yet (port from the cited source, recoverable with `git show "bd92373^:<path>"`, and register the port in the catalog).

**Never invent a component.** The order is fixed and there is no fourth option:

1. **The catalog has it** — import it. If it almost fits, EXTEND it (a prop, a mode) rather than writing a parallel one; the `StatTile` filter mode exists because a screen needed a clickable tile and the first attempt was eleven hand-written boxes instead.
2. **The template has it and it is not packaged yet** — port it from the real source, keeping its shape: radius, tint (`-light/10`), band height, type scale, hover. Recover it with `git show "bd92373^:apps/web/src/app/(dashboard)/dashboards/..."`. Register the port in `catalog.json` in the same commit.
3. **Neither has it** — build it, but build it IN the template's language: the same tokens, the same radii, the same tone vocabulary (`TILE_TONE`), the same type scale. Put it in `components/product/` with a comment saying which template section it is closest to and why nothing existing fit. Then register it.

A `<Box>` with a border and a radius is not a shortcut, it is a second design language growing next to the template's. It type-checks, it survives review, and it is invisible until someone opens the screen and asks why it looks nothing like the rest. `product-lint` blocks the interactive form of this (`[invented-component]`) and flags the static one.

**The check is about SHAPE, not only tokens.** For every value you are about to render, ask which entry fits it:

| The data is | The component |
| --- | --- |
| a series the payload already carries | `TrendSparkline` |
| an x-of-y allowance | `UsageMeter` |
| 2–6 counters describing one population | `MetricBars` |
| a single counter, or a clickable filter | `StatTile` |
| a lifecycle state | `ConsultationStatusChip` (pastel badge — the state is NAMED, never colour alone) |
| where one thing stands, in a list of things | a state line: the state's icon in its colour and the state in words, on the right of the row (not packaged yet: build it under rule 3) |
| where something stands along its way | a progress rail of steps for the whole, a thin bar in a row, a list of the latest few (not packaged yet: build them under rule 3) |
| a definition attached to a number | `InfoHint` (a `Tooltip` on a plain `Box` dies on touch and keyboard) |
| what a page, card or section is for | its title component's `description`, behind the "i" (never a paragraph under the title) |
| a section with nothing in it | `EmptyState` with an `illustration`, never a grey line over a blank |
| zero data | `EmptyState`, or the illustrated banner when the whole screen is empty |

A series, a ratio, a share or a counter set rendered as a **sentence of numbers** is a defect — it makes the reader do arithmetic the component would have done. Equally a defect: hand-rolling a shape the catalog already has, and hardcoding `bg-primary/10` where `TILE_TONE` prescribes `bg-{tone}-light/10`.

The inverse is also a defect: **never invent a chart to fill space.** A visual earns its place only when it answers a question the user actually asks, on data that already exists. Below ~6 categories `MetricBars` beats a donut; without real prior-period data there is no trend to draw.

## Reusable building blocks

`apps/web/src/components/product/`: `EmptyState`, `OnboardingChecklist`, `OnboardingChecklistCard`, `ActivationProgress`, `SetupWizard`. State persistence: `@flyee/onboarding` (`getOnboardingState`, `completeStep`, `computeProgress`) over `onboarding_state` (migration 0009). Step definitions live in the project's code; only state is persisted.

**Live predicates are the preferred path** (not click-tracking): give a step a `done` derived from real product state (`{ key, title, done: hasConnection }`) so the checklist reflects reality; `completeStep` is only for steps with no observable signal. Strings in these components come from the `product` i18n namespace (all 6 locales). For multi-tenant setup work, set `ONBOARDING_ORG_SCOPED = true` so activation is shared across an org's members instead of per user.

**Wiring (already in place):** declare the steps in `apps/web/src/lib/onboarding.ts` (`ONBOARDING_STEPS`; empty ⇒ everything degrades to the app root). The four auth entry points (sign-in, sign-up, `/auth/callback`, `/auth/two-factor`) call `resolvePostAuthDestination`, sending a user with pending onboarding to `/onboarding` (the `SetupWizard`, self-guarding) and everyone else to `DEFAULTS.appRoot`. The app home mounts `OnboardingChecklistCard` for the remaining steps. Do NOT put this check in the middleware — it runs on every request and must stay query-free.

## Before finishing

Walk the screen as a brand-new user (empty data) AND as a returning one. Confirm: no blank empty states; a first-run path to value exists; no CRUD table where a focused view fits the job better; no task left to the person that the product could do, and nothing the product does listed as theirs; every card asks only for what is still owed and says each thing's state in words; every count matches the list it labels; **every value is in the catalog shape that fits it — no series, ratio or counter set left as a sentence, and no chart added that the data does not support**; nothing typed can be lost; strings translated; tokens only; `npm run build` + `npm run lint:fix` pass.

For a new flow or a substantial edit, run the `product-verify` skill — it drives the real app through the journey (new + returning user, 1440/390, light/dark) and judges dead ends, lost context, error-vs-empty and leftover demo surfaces. Code that was never run is not verified.
